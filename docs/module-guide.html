Consent lifecycle

The daily work: collect a choice, store it, check it, let the customer manage it. Seven modules.

2

Eight different front doors — app, web, WhatsApp, branch, call centre, email link, QR, partner API. Whichever the customer uses, the platform writes the identical record with the same strength of proof. The wizard is five steps and refuses shortcuts.

Meera opens an account at a branch. The officer runs the wizard on a tablet: she sees the notice in Tamil, ticks account servicing and loan processing but leaves marketing off, verifies by OTP, and gets a receipt on her phone.

Go and ask a group of customers for consent. Pick a purpose, an audience and the channels to reach them on, then watch responses come back. It is a compliance tool, not a marketing campaign tool — no budgets, no A/B tests.

5
Consent Health
One honest question: how much of the consent you hold can you actually rely on today? Everything else — expired, withdrawn, still pending — cannot be used. One score, four numbers, and the list of what lapses next.
For example

6
Validation Engine ★ DEAL-WINNER
The bouncer at the door. Before any system uses a customer's data, it asks one question and gets one answer in milliseconds. If consent cannot be proven — or anything goes wrong — the answer is no. Silence is never a yes.
For example
The CRM wants to email Meera. It asks: may I use Meera's data for marketing? Answer: BLOCK — consent withdrawn 14 Jul. But when core banking asks about KYC, the answer is ALLOW — that runs on legal obligation, which she cannot withdraw from.

7
Privacy Center
The requests desk. When a customer exercises a right — show me my data, correct it, delete it, raise a complaint, nominate someone — it lands here with a clock ticking, runs as a fan-out across systems, and ends in a certificate proving it was done.
For example
Meera asks for a copy of everything held about her. The platform first verifies it is really her by OTP, then collects from eight systems, and delivers a machine-readable file plus a completion certificate — inside the response window.

Preference Center ★ DEAL-WINNER
The only screen your customer ever sees. Your brand, their language, their phone — a simple list of switches, one per purpose. Two lines of code embed it in your existing app. Flipping a switch here is identical in effect to any other channel.

Automation

The machinery that carries a decision everywhere, without anyone lifting a finger. Two modules.

9
Orchestration ★★ THE MONEY SHOT
The messenger. One consent change fans out to every system that holds that customer's data, in real time. Most acknowledge. Some retry. And when one genuinely fails, it is caught in a queue with a person's name on it — never silently dropped.
For example
Meera withdraws marketing. Seven systems stop within 420ms. The SMS gateway times out but succeeds on retry. The data warehouse fails — its credentials expired — and drops into the dead-letter queue, assigned to the DPO.
🟢 stopped (7)🟠 retried🔴 failed → queued
10
Workflow Builder
Where a compliance officer sets the rules — by clicking, not coding. It reads like a recipe card: WHEN this happens, THEN do these steps, and IF a step fails, do this. A Test Run walks through it in green without touching anything live.
For example
The DPO builds: WHEN a consent is 30 days from expiry, THEN send a renewal notice, WAIT 7 days, and IF still no reply, escalate to me. No developer involved. It now runs automatically for every expiring consent.

Intelligence

The layer that watches for trouble a human would miss. One module.

15
AI Compliance
The night-shift analyst. It watches everything and flags the strange things a person would never spot — unusual volumes, odd logins, consents given suspiciously fast. It proposes; a human always decides. It never acts alone.
For example
A campaign pulls 84,000 profiles at 2am against a normal baseline of 2,000. It is flagged within minutes. Separately, one branch averages 18-second consents — meaning nobody there is actually reading the notice to customers.

Governance

The heavier duties — used rarely, and they have to work. Six modules.

16
Breach Response
The emergency room. A breach is not really a security event — it is a reporting event with a clock on it. This runs the countdown, works out exactly who was affected from the evidence vault, drafts the notices, and assembles the report. Then it stops: a human signs it, not a machine.
For example
A vendor leak is detected at 09:12. The clock starts. The platform computes 12,480 affected customers from actual records — not an estimate — drafts notices in nine languages, and assembles the Board report. The DPO reviews and signs.

17
Children's Data
The special-care ward. A child cannot legally consent — a verified guardian must, and the platform has to prove that guardian was real. A tick-box saying "I am a parent" is refused. Marketing, profiling and tracking are hard-blocked for every minor, and no role can override it.
For example
Anika is 17 with a youth account. Her guardian was never verified, so the platform blocks 8 processing purposes automatically. Vihaan turns 18 in 22 days — the platform will invite him to re-consent in his own right.

18
SDF Pack
Extra paperwork for the big players. Large organisations get designated Significant Data Fiduciaries, which switches on two additional duties: a formal impact assessment, and an independent audit every year. Includes a workspace where an outside auditor can inspect without ever seeing raw personal data.
For example
The bank is designated an SDF. Its DPIA is complete and approved; the independent audit is marked "not yet commissioned" — because it hasn't been. The platform shows the truth rather than a green tick.

20
Developers
The workshop. Where the bank's own engineers get what they need to wire Consentia into their systems — the API, sample keys, webhooks, SDKs and the embeddable widget. It signals "we are built to plug into your world", which is what a technical buyer checks before trusting anything.
For example
A developer adds one call before the CRM sends any email. If it returns BLOCK, the send never happens. That is the entire integration — one question, asked every time.

21
Settings
The control panel — the bank's own details, its notice text, its languages, and its enforcement defaults. Importantly, the response-time windows are set by the bank's own lawyers, not hard-coded by us, and are clearly marked until counsel confirms them.
For example
The rights-response window shows "awaiting counsel sign-off" until the bank's legal team confirms the number. The platform never pretends to have decided the law on their behalf.
